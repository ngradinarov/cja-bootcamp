Making the Most of Your Annual Wellness Visit

MR
Marcus Rivera  ·  RN, Health Navigator
| 3 min read | February 3, 2026

Your annual wellness visit is one of the most valuable benefits included in your AcmeHealth plan, and yet it's one of the most frequently overlooked. Unlike a sick visit, a wellness exam is about prevention — catching potential issues before they become problems, updating your health history, and getting personalized guidance from your care team when you're feeling your best. Understanding how to prepare for and participate in this visit will help you leave with a clearer picture of your health and a stronger relationship with your doctor.

Before your appointment, spend 10 minutes reviewing what's changed in your health since your last visit. Have you noticed any new symptoms, even minor ones? Has anything changed in your family history — a parent or sibling diagnosed with heart disease, diabetes, or cancer? Writing these down beforehand means nothing gets lost in the moment. Also compile a list of every medication, supplement, and vitamin you're currently taking, including over-the-counter products, because these interactions matter to your care team.

During the visit itself, be direct about your concerns. Doctors appreciate patients who come prepared with specific questions: "I've been sleeping poorly for three months — what should we check?" or "I have a family history of colon cancer — is it time to talk about screening?" Wellness visits typically include vital sign checks, a review of preventive screenings that are due (mammograms, colonoscopies, blood pressure, cholesterol), and an opportunity to address mental health and lifestyle factors. Don't wait for the doctor to bring everything up — this is your time.

Screenings covered during your annual wellness visit vary by age, sex, and health history, but most AcmeHealth plans cover a standard panel of preventive services at no cost to you. This includes blood pressure and cholesterol checks, diabetes screenings, depression screening, and age-appropriate cancer screenings. After the visit, ask for a written summary of what was discussed and any referrals or follow-up steps. Keeping your own record creates continuity across years of care.

Key Takeaway: Coming prepared with a written list of medications, recent changes, and specific questions turns a 20-minute visit into a genuinely productive health conversation. Don't let that time go to small talk.

Your annual visit is not just a checkbox — it's an investment in catching things early. The research is clear: people who stay current on preventive care have better long-term health outcomes, spend less on reactive treatment, and report higher confidence in managing their own health. Schedule yours within the first quarter of each year so it stays top of mind, and log in to your member portal to see which screenings your plan covers at no additional cost.
