Understanding Your Prescription Drug Coverage

LC
Lisa Chen  ·  PharmD, Clinical Pharmacist
| 4 min read | January 20, 2026

Prescription drug coverage is one of the most used — and most misunderstood — components of any health plan. Even members who carefully reviewed their coverage during enrollment often find themselves confused when they reach the pharmacy counter. Understanding how your plan structures drug coverage can save you money, reduce frustration, and help you make informed decisions about your medications. The key concepts to understand are: formularies, drug tiers, prior authorization, and quantity limits.

A formulary is your plan's approved list of covered drugs. If a medication your doctor prescribes is on the formulary, it will be covered (subject to your cost-sharing). If it's not on the formulary, you may need to pay full price or ask your doctor to prescribe an alternative. Formularies are not static — they can change annually, which is why reviewing your plan's drug list each year during open enrollment is important, especially if you take ongoing medications. AcmeHealth's formulary is available in your member portal, and a pharmacist or member services representative can help you look up any specific drug.

Most formularies organize covered drugs into tiers, with lower tiers representing lower cost-sharing. Tier 1 typically includes generic drugs — the least expensive option. Tier 2 covers preferred brand-name drugs. Tier 3 covers non-preferred brands. Higher tiers may include specialty drugs for complex conditions, which can carry significant cost-sharing. Asking your doctor whether a generic equivalent is available — or whether a Tier 1 alternative could work equally well — is always worth the conversation. In many cases, generics are therapeutically equivalent to brand names at a fraction of the cost.

Prior authorization is a process by which your plan requires your doctor to confirm that a specific medication is medically necessary before it will be covered. If your pharmacy tells you a drug requires prior authorization, contact your doctor's office — they typically handle this on your behalf with clinical documentation. Quantity limits may also apply, restricting how many pills or refills you can receive within a given period. If a quantity limit conflicts with your prescribed dosage, your doctor can submit an exception request.

Key Takeaway: If a drug seems more expensive than expected at the pharmacy, don't simply pay and leave. Ask the pharmacist: Is there a generic? Is there a lower-tier version? Has prior authorization been submitted? Small questions at the counter can save you significant money.

Mail-order pharmacy programs, available through most AcmeHealth plans, can reduce the per-unit cost of maintenance medications and offer the convenience of home delivery with 90-day supplies. If you take a medication daily for an ongoing condition, it's worth asking whether mail order would save you money. Your member portal or member services line can walk you through your specific options — and a clinical pharmacist consultation is often a covered benefit that many members never use.
