Divider.

Real-Time CDP

Profiles, audiences, destinations

  • Profile. One record per person, assembled from every dataset enabled for profile, keyed by the identity graph.
  • Audience. A set of people who meet a definition, evaluated on a schedule or in a stream.
  • Destination. Where an audience is sent: an advertising platform, an email system, cloud storage, or another Adobe application.
  • Merge policy. The rule deciding which value wins when two datasets disagree about the same attribute.
Merge policies are easy to ignore and they decide what a profile says. If membership says Silver and the web session says Gold, the merge policy picks the winner, and every audience built on plan inherits that decision.
Five minutes. Merge policy is the concept most likely to bite this audience in production and most likely to be skipped in training, so give it the callout. Keep the rest brisk; the lab is where RTCDP becomes concrete.

Divider.

Journey Optimizer

Journeys, campaigns and channels

  • A journey is triggered and stateful. Someone enters, waits, branches on a condition, receives something, and exits. It reacts to a person over time.
  • A campaign is a single send to an audience at a point in time. No waiting, no branching.
  • Channels are email, SMS, push, in-app and web. The web channel is the one that matters to us.
  • Entry can be an event, a scheduled read of an audience, or qualification for an audience in real time.
Journey for "when this happens to this person, do this next". Campaign for "send this to these people on Thursday". The distinction is examinable in the Journey Optimizer certification, not this one, but it is the first thing a stakeholder will ask you.
Six minutes. Keep it conceptual; the room is not being certified on AJO. The journey against campaign distinction is the one they will be asked about by colleagues, so it earns the callout. Read-audience entry is what the lab uses, so name it clearly.

Journey Optimizer

Where your audience shows up

  • Audiences published from CJA appear in Journey Optimizer's audience list with no additional configuration.
  • A read audience journey pulls the membership on a schedule and enters those people.
  • An audience qualification trigger enters people as they join, for streaming audiences.
  • Ours is a one-time publish, so a read audience is the right entry for today.
No connector, no export, no file transfer. The audience is in the profile store, and Journey Optimizer reads the same store.
Four minutes. The absence of integration work is the point and it lands best if you contrast it with what this would take in a conventional stack, which for this audience is usually a file drop and a two week wait.

Journey Optimizer

The web channel, and the AcmeHealth slots

  • The web channel delivers content into a page through the same Web SDK that collects your data.
  • The page exposes named slots. AcmeHealth has four empty containers waiting: ajo-banner and ajo-pharmacy-banner on the home page, ajo-offer on appointments, ajo-offer-doctor on find a doctor.
  • A web experience targets a slot, for an audience, with a piece of content.
  • The impression is collected as an event, so it comes back into CJA.
The same SDK call that told you 836 people were stuck is the one that will deliver the message to them. Collection and personalisation are one pipe.
Five minutes. The one-pipe point is genuinely important architecture and it explains why the slots exist in the demo site at all. Show the empty divs in the page source if you have a moment; it makes the abstraction concrete.

Journey Optimizer

Closing the loop

  • Journey Optimizer reports on its own sends, opens and clicks.
  • CJA reports on what those people did afterwards, across every channel.
  • The journey canvas visualization can read a journey defined in Journey Optimizer and show fallout through it.
  • So the question "did the intervention work" is answered in CJA, not in the tool that ran the intervention.
This is the argument for analytics sitting outside the campaign tool. A campaign tool grades its own homework. CJA sees the member's whole week.
Four minutes. The homework line is the memorable framing and it is the honest case for CJA alongside AJO rather than instead of it. Connect back to Wednesday's canvas so they recognise it as the same visualization.

Brand Visibility

From LLM Optimizer to Adobe Brand Visibility

  • Adobe shipped LLM Optimizer in October 2025, to measure and improve how brands appear in AI generated answers.
  • At Summit in April 2026 Adobe announced a brand visibility solution spanning Experience Manager, LLM Optimizer and Brand Concierge.
  • In June 2026 it was announced as Adobe Brand Visibility, combining LLM Optimizer with the Semrush AI search database acquired that April.
  • If you go looking for LLM Optimizer now, you land on Brand Visibility. Same product lineage, new name and a much larger prompt dataset behind it.
Worth knowing the history because the documentation, the training material and the sales conversation are all currently using both names.
Six minutes. Somebody in the room will have heard one name and not the other, so the timeline is genuinely useful rather than trivia. Do not oversell; this is a young product category and the naming is still moving.

Brand Visibility

Generative engine optimisation

  • Monitors how your brand appears in answers generated by assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Perplexity.
  • Reports on presence, accuracy and citation: are you mentioned, are you described correctly, are you the source.
  • Produces prescriptive content recommendations, and can automate some fixes.
  • The premise: if a member asks an assistant before they ever reach your site, being cited is the new ranking.
For a retailer this is a revenue question. For a health plan it is an accuracy and trust question first. If an assistant tells a member a drug is covered when it is not, that is a member harm before it is a marketing problem.
Six minutes. The healthcare reframe in the callout is what makes this relevant rather than a generic SEO pitch, and it is the version of the argument this room will repeat internally. Invite examples: formulary, network coverage, prior authorisation rules are all things members ask assistants about.

Brand Visibility

Why this is a trust problem, not an SEO problem

  • Members increasingly ask an assistant before they open a plan website.
  • What the assistant says about coverage, formulary, network or eligibility is treated as the plan's answer.
  • If it is wrong, the consequence is a member decision made on bad information, not a lost click.
  • Measuring it is the first step. You cannot correct what you cannot see.
The analyst's contribution here is not optimisation. It is putting a number on how often the assistant is wrong about your plan, and getting that number in front of someone who can fix the content.
Five minutes. This is the slide that justifies the section to a sceptical analyst. Keep it short and let the room react; several of them will have already been asked about AI answers by a stakeholder and will not have had a way to respond.

Content Analytics

Which content actually did the work

  • An add-on to CJA that identifies the individual assets and experiences on a page and measures each one's contribution.
  • Automatically detects images and experience elements rather than requiring manual tagging of every asset.
  • Attributes engagement and conversion to specific content, so you can answer which image, which headline, which module.
  • Supports web, and now native iOS and Android apps.
  • Reports inside Analysis Workspace, using the same components and segments you have been using all week.
For AcmeHealth the obvious question is the select PCP page. Something on it is failing 836 people, and standard web analytics can tell you the page failed but not which part of it.
Eight minutes. The callout connects it to the week's finding and shows the genuine gap: they have narrowed the problem to one page and they still cannot say what on the page is wrong. That is the honest limit of what they built this week, and naming it is better than pretending the analysis is complete.

CJA for B2B

When the customer is an organisation

  • Adds account, opportunity and buying group structures on top of the person level model.
  • Lets you report at the account level: which organisations are engaged, not just which individuals.
  • Relevant to Kaiser Permanente through employer and group plans, where the buyer is a benefits team rather than a member.
  • Licensed separately, as an add-on measured in reportable businessperson profiles.
Named here because it exists and because the employer channel is real for this organisation. It is not part of this week's environment.

Certification

How the questions are written

  • Mostly scenario based. A business situation, then "what should the analyst do" or "which option meets the requirement".
  • Multiple choice with one answer, and some multiple select where the question tells you how many to pick.
  • Distractors are usually plausible but wrong for a stated reason, not obviously silly.
  • The stated detail matters. If the scenario says the events are weeks apart, that is there to rule out session scope.
  • Very few questions test a definition in isolation. Most test whether you can choose between two reasonable options.
Read the scenario twice before the options. Almost every question contains one detail that eliminates two answers, and it is usually a timeframe, a container or a data source.
Six minutes. The callout is the single most useful exam technique for this paper. Give a worked example out loud: "members who called and later completed the checklist" contains "later", which rules out anything session scoped, and that single word decides the question.
